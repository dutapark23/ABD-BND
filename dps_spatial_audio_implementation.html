The Critical Distinction You Need to Understand

The Most Important Technical Clarification in This Document

When people say "Dolby Atmos room certification," they mean a full cinema/music production facility — a 7.1.4 speaker array, treated room, Dolby site visit, months of work, and significant investment. That is NOT what DPS needs.

For podcasts, the correct format is binaural spatial audio — a software-only process that creates full 3D immersive audio delivered as a standard stereo file. It works on every pair of headphones, on every platform, with no special equipment needed by the listener. This is what Apple AirPods, Spotify, and YouTube support. And it can be set up tomorrow.

What Binaural Spatial Audio Sounds Like

Instead of everything coming from "inside your head" as mono/stereo audio does on headphones — binaural processing places sounds in specific positions in 3D space around the listener. The host voice comes from slightly in front. The guest's voice comes from 30 degrees to the right. Room ambience wraps around from behind. On AirPods with head-tracking enabled, the sound stays anchored to the virtual room even when the listener turns their head. It is a fundamentally different, more immersive experience — and once someone hears it, they notice the difference immediately.

02 — Days 2–3

The Standard Spatial Mix Template

This is the exact spatial audio positioning template to use for every DPS podcast recording. Once it is set up as a session template, applying it to any new recording takes 5 minutes.

The DPS Spatial Audio Positioning Standard

These positions are based on how humans naturally perceive conversation. The listener feels like they are in the room with the hosts — a physically present experience that is completely absent from standard stereo podcast audio.

Spatial Positioning Map — Listener's Perspective (Top View)
Host Voice (Primary)
Position: Centre, 1.5m forward, head height
This anchors the listener to the conversation. The main voice should always feel "in front" — commanding and clear.
Guest Voice
Position: 30° right, 1.2m, head height
Natural seating position — feels like the guest is across the table. For video pods, match the physical camera angle.
Second Guest (if any)
Position: 30° left, 1.2m, head height
Mirrored from Guest 1. Listener feels seated between two people at a table.
Room Ambience / Reverb
Position: 360° sphere, distance 3m, -18dB
The DPS studio acoustic signature. Very low level but completely wraps the listener. This is what makes the room feel real — not just voices in a void.
Intro/Outro Music
Position: Wide stereo, slightly behind, -6dB
Music should feel like it comes from behind and around the listener — immersive bed under the vocal performance, not competing with it.
Ad Reads (if any)
Position: Centre, forward, slightly closer than host
Ad reads should feel like the host is leaning toward the listener — closer, more intimate. This subtle shift unconsciously signals "important message."
