Section 01

The Honest Diagnosis

Before any strategy can work, the situation must be named accurately. There are three forces at work simultaneously, and each requires a different response.

Force 01 — External Demand Shock

The regional conflict has materially reduced international visitor volume to Dubai. This is not a marketing problem. No amount of clever advertising brings back visitors who have chosen not to travel to the region. This force cannot be fixed. It must be navigated around. The strategy must stop relying on visitor-dependent revenue and pivot toward audiences that are already in Dubai — or coming despite the broader climate.

Force 02 — Seasonal Compression

Summer in Dubai is historically the weakest period for resident and expat activity. Expats leave for home countries. The heat keeps people indoors. Event calendars empty. This is a known, predictable pattern — and the correct response is not to fight it, but to substitute the audience that leaves (Western expats) with the audience that arrives (Gulf nationals). This is the single most underused strategic insight in Dubai's hospitality and services sector.

Force 03 — Competitive Price Erosion

Competitors are cutting prices to survive. This creates pressure to follow. Do not follow. Price wars initiated by weaker players are a trap designed to bring the leader down to their level. A studio that cuts prices in a quality-signals market tells every prospective client that their prices were arbitrary to begin with — and that the quality claims may also be. Your Google Reviews leadership position is incompatible with being the cheapest option. They cannot coexist.

Core Strategic Principle

The goal is not to fight on three fronts simultaneously. It is to selectively withdraw from the one front you cannot win (visitor market recovery), hold firm on the front that protects margin (pricing), and go on the offensive on the two fronts where you have genuine advantage — the resident/corporate market and the Gulf nationals summer market.

A Note on the Team Reality

This strategy is built around the team you actually have — not the team you wish you had. The operators are loyal, consistent, and reliable. They are not creative directors. Every new service proposed in this document is specifically designed to be executable without creative judgment. Consistency is your competitive advantage. Never sacrifice it chasing complexity.

Section 02

Competitive Intelligence — Poddster

Poddster is the primary competitor in the Dubai podcast studio market. They operate at dubai.poddster.com with 9 studios across 4 locations: Al Barsha, Dubai Media City, Motor City, and Palm Jumeirah. Notably, none of these locations are in Business Bay — which means DPS holds a geographic monopoly on the most commercially dense address in the city.

The Direct Assessment

Poddster is building a wide ecosystem at the deliberate cost of margin. They are investing in community, education, SEO content, multiple locations, and corporate enterprise sales simultaneously. This is an expensive strategy. The founder's assessment that they barely break even is likely accurate. But "barely breaking even" does not mean irrelevant — it means they are prioritising long-term market position over short-term profit. In a recovering market, that bet could pay off significantly. DPS must be aware of this trajectory.

What Poddster Has That DPS Does Not

Threat Level — High

78 Blog Articles — SEO Machine

Poddster has published 78 SEO-targeted articles covering every keyword in the podcast studio space. This is a deliberate organic search strategy that quietly redirects discovery traffic away from DPS. This gap will compound over time if left unaddressed.

Threat Level — High

Enterprise Corporate Portfolio

Microsoft, Amazon, PwC, Emirates NBD, Visa, Nestlé, NET-A-PORTER. 50+ global brands. 240M lifetime podcast views across their client base. This is a proven, operating B2B revenue machine that DPS currently has no equivalent to.

Threat Level — Medium

PodClub + Poddster Academy

Community membership and bi-monthly masterclasses create retention and referral loops that pure transactional studios cannot match. Once a creator is embedded in PodClub, switching feels like a social cost, not just a commercial one.

Threat Level — Medium

Anghami is the dominant audio streaming platform across the Arab world. Poddster offers promotional placement (Homepage Feature, Search Boost, Push Notifications) at 440–730 AED. For Arabic-language content creators, this is a distribution layer DPS cannot currently match.

4 locations across Dubai. For clients in Al Barsha or Motor City, Poddster is simply closer. DPS cannot address this without capital investment in new locations. The response is to make Business Bay proximity a premium argument, not to try to match coverage.

Section 03

What Must Not Change

Before addressing what needs to shift, it is equally important to be explicit about what must be protected. These are not negotiable — touching them would cost more than the revenue they might temporarily generate.

Protect

The #1 Google Reviews Position

You are number one in the region, not just Dubai. This is your most durable competitive moat. Every decision must be filtered through this question: "Does this risk a negative review?" If yes, don't do it.

Protect

The "Record and Leave" Brand Promise

Clients chose you because you deliver without drama. Reliability over creativity. "You come any day of the week and always get what we promise." This is rare and valuable. Don't abandon it to compete with Poddster's community model.

Protect

The Price Floor

450 AED/hour for video + audio is already competitive for Business Bay with your equipment. Discounting the base rate signals weakness and erodes brand perception. Do not lower it. Create different packages instead.

Protect

Standardised Service Delivery

Customisation creates demanding clients and inconsistent reviews. The decision to stay in the mass-market, standardised model was correct. Any new service must be template-based and repeatable.

The Poddster Trap: Do not attempt to build a community to compete with Poddster. Your venue is not sized for it, your audience DNA is not built for it, and your team is not resourced for it. Poddster's website is currently unreachable — their model may not be as strong as it appears. Competitors in the community space often sacrifice margin for engagement. You chose margin. That was correct.
Section 04

The Three Markets to Win Right Now

The visitor market is diminished for the foreseeable future. The strategy must pivot to the three segments that are present, active, and spending regardless of the regional climate.

Market 01 — Dubai Residents: Coaches, Consultants, Entrepreneurs

Dubai has a large, permanent population of coaches, business consultants, and entrepreneurs who are building personal brands. They are not visiting — they live here. They need content every week. This segment is almost entirely immune to the visitor market decline. This is the most stable, recurring revenue opportunity available to you right now.

  • Target via LinkedIn and Instagram with messaging about consistent, professional podcast production
  • Offer a monthly recording membership (detailed in Section 06) to convert one-time clients into predictable monthly revenue
  • Leverage the coaching vertical page you already have — it needs updating and a direct call to book a recurring package

Market 02 — Corporate & B2B

Companies in Business Bay and DIFC do not travel. They are open. They produce content year-round. A corporate client on a monthly programme is worth ten times a one-off creator booking. This segment requires a different sales approach — direct outreach, not digital ads — but it is the highest-value segment in the building.

  • Target: Communications Directors, PR Managers, CMOs at mid-to-large companies in Business Bay and DIFC
  • Channel: LinkedIn direct outreach + email
  • Offer: A "Corporate Podcast Programme" package (detailed in Section 06)
  • Proof: The fact that major international figures have recorded at your studios is powerful for corporate buyers who value credibility signals

Market 03 — The French-Speaking Creator Ecosystem

Yomi Denzel and Oussama Amar — two of the most followed French-speaking entrepreneurs in the world — have recorded at your studios. The French-speaking creator economy spans France, Morocco, Tunisia, Algeria, Ivory Coast, Senegal, and beyond. Many of these creators visit Dubai specifically for content and business. This is a market that can be activated at near-zero cost through the relationships you already have.

  • One social post from either Yomi Denzel or Oussama Amar mentioning the studio reaches millions of exactly the right audience
  • Create a French-language version of the booking page or at minimum French-language ads targeting this demographic
  • Target French-speaking creator communities on Instagram and YouTube with awareness content featuring these names
Section 05

The Summer Strategy — What Actually Works

Corrected Assessment

The initial assumption that Gulf nationals travel to Dubai in summer was wrong — client intelligence from direct contact with the studio's own Saudi and Kuwaiti clients confirms they are travelling to Bali, Europe, and Thailand during the summer months, not staying in the region. The summer strategy must be built around audiences that are genuinely present, not assumed ones.

Who Is Actually in Dubai in Summer

The summer audience is smaller but real. It consists of three groups:

  • Long-term residents who cannot or do not travel — professionals in their first or second year in Dubai, people with ongoing business commitments, those who simply stay. This group is present, under-marketed to, and more accessible than ever because the city is quieter.
  • Corporate teams with Q3 content deadlines — companies don't go on summer holiday. Their communications and marketing teams still have deliverables. A summer slow-down for consumers is not a slow-down for B2B content production.
  • International visitors who still come despite the climate — not in volume, but in quality. A creator who chooses to visit Dubai in July despite the heat is highly motivated. They are coming specifically to work, not for leisure. These are the highest-intent visitors you will see all year.

The Summer Positioning Play

Turn the slow season into a selling point. "Dubai is quieter in summer — which means the studio is too. Priority booking, faster availability, no waiting." Frame low utilisation as an exclusive experience, not a sign of weakness. This is not dishonest — it is true. And it speaks directly to the professional creator who values access and efficiency over energy and buzz.

Summer Move 01

B2B Summer Sprint

Push the corporate outreach harder in June–August precisely because decision-makers are less distracted by events and social obligations. Summer is when corporate buyers have more capacity to respond to a well-timed outreach.

Summer Move 02

Membership Conversion Drive

Offer a summer membership sign-up bonus — "Sign up for a 3-month membership before July 1 and get the first month at 20% off." Lock in recurring clients before the slow season, not after it. The discount is recovered within the second month.

Summer Move 03

Past Client Re-Engagement

"Summer is the perfect time to finally launch that podcast. The studio is available — are you?" A single WhatsApp message to all past clients who booked more than 6 months ago. Low cost, direct, timely.

Summer Move 04

SEO Content Acceleration

Publish aggressively in summer. Blog articles indexed in June–August will be live and ranking when the September-October peak season returns. Summer's slower business pace is the window to build the SEO foundation that pays off in Q4.

Section 06

Revenue Recovery Architecture

Three principles govern every new offer below. If a proposed service fails any one of them, it does not belong in the lineup.

  • Deliverable by the current team without creative judgment — no offer should rely on the operator deciding what looks or sounds good
  • Standardised and repeatable — the same inputs produce the same output every time, protecting the Google Reviews position
  • Additive to cash flow, not destructive to margin — new offers must generate revenue above the cost of delivery

New Offer 01 — The Monthly Recording Membership

This is the most important single change you can make. Convert irregular bookings into predictable monthly revenue.

Why this works for the team: A member client is a known client. The operator knows their setup preferences, their voice, their camera angle. Zero learning curve per session. This actually makes the operator's job easier and the output more consistent.

New Offer 02 — AI Reels Add-On (The Right Way to Implement It)

The owner correctly identified this as an opportunity. The obstacle — team creativity and taste — is solved by removing the human creative decision entirely. The algorithm decides, not the operator.

The tool: Opus Clip (or Descript). These AI tools automatically identify the most engaging 60–90 second moments from a session recording and generate short-form clips with captions. The operator uploads the file. The AI selects and cuts. The operator exports. No taste required.

New Offer 04 — Express Raw File Delivery

Currently raw files are delivered in 2–3 days. This creates friction for creators who operate on tight content schedules. A simple tiered delivery model converts this constraint into revenue.

  • Standard delivery: 1–2 days — included in all packages (no change)
  • Express delivery (same day, within 4 hours): +150 AED — operator transfers directly at end of session via high-speed upload link

This requires no creative skill. It requires one operator to stay at the machine for 30 minutes post-session to ensure upload completion. At 150 AED per upsell, and if 20% of sessions opt in, it adds meaningful revenue with zero additional overhead.

What NOT to Add

Do not add: Full creative editing packages beyond what you currently offer, podcast strategy consulting, social media management, or any service that requires the team to exercise creative judgment on behalf of the client. Every time you have offered this, demanding clients have followed. The mass-market, consistent-delivery model is the asset. Do not compromise it.

Paid & Organic Channel Strategy

With a reduced team and tighter budget, channel selection must be ruthless. Do not spread across all platforms. Do fewer things significantly better.

Channels — Priority Order

Google Ads
Priority 1 — Immediate, high-intent traffic. "Podcast studio Dubai," "record podcast Dubai," "video podcast studio business bay" are searched by people ready to book. This is the most direct path from ad spend to booking confirmation. Budget: 3,500–5,000 AED/month. Start here before any other paid channel.
Google Reviews
Priority 1 — Protect the moat actively. You are #1 in the region. Every session should end with a verbal request for a Google review. Train every operator to say it naturally at the end of each session. This is your most powerful marketing asset and it costs zero to maintain — only effort.
Snapchat Ads
Priority 1 (June–September only) — Gulf nationals summer play. Arabic-language ads targeting Gulf national visitors in Dubai. This is uncontested territory in your category. Budget: 2,000–3,000 AED/month across summer. Measure cost per booking inquiry. If it works in June, scale in July.
Instagram
Priority 2 — Brand presence and booking conversion. Studio footage, equipment walkthroughs, "behind the session" content. 3 Reels per week minimum. The visual quality of your studios sells itself — the operator just needs to point the phone at the room. No creative direction required.
LinkedIn
Priority 2 — B2B and corporate pipeline. Direct outreach to Communications Directors, CMOs, and PR managers in Business Bay and DIFC. 20 personalised messages per week. Content: "How Dubai's top professionals are using podcasts to build authority." This is the right audience, in the right building, and LinkedIn reaches them directly.
WhatsApp
Priority 2 — Re-engagement of past clients. Your most underused channel for the most valuable audience you have — people who have already paid you. A single WhatsApp broadcast to past clients costs nothing. Results can be immediate. This is covered fully in Section 08.
TikTok
Priority 3 — Discovery and reach. Valuable but lower priority given team constraints. If content is already being shot for Instagram, repurpose it to TikTok. Do not create TikTok-specific content with the current team size.

What to Post — The No-Creativity Rule

Given team limitations, all social content must require zero creative judgment. Every piece of content falls into one of these five categories:

Content Type 01

Studio Footage

Point a phone at the studio before/during a session. Cameras. Lights. Microphones. The room sells itself. No script, no concept, no creative direction needed.

Content Type 02

Client Permission Clips

At the start of each session, ask the client if you may share a 10-second clip on Instagram. Most will say yes. One operator records 10 seconds. Upload same day.

Content Type 03

Equipment Features

"This is the same microphone Joe Rogan uses." "These cameras are Netflix-approved." Product facts delivered directly. No styling needed.

Content Type 04

Review Reposts

Screenshot every 5-star Google review and post it to Instagram Stories. Costs 2 minutes. Serves as constant social proof reinforcement.

Section 09

On Pricing — The Race You Must Not Enter

The Pricing Principle

You are the leader. Leaders do not price-match struggling competitors. When weaker players lower prices to survive, they are signalling desperation — not value. If you match them, you tell the market that your higher prices were unjustified. You will never recover that perception.

What You Can Do Instead of Lowering Prices

1

Create a True Entry Point

A first-time client "Welcome Session" — 1 hour video + audio for 350 AED (vs 450 standard). Available once per new client only. This feels like a discount without diluting the ongoing rate. New clients experience the studio at lower risk. Conversion to repeat bookings at full rate should be high given the product quality.

1

Increase Perceived Value, Not Discounts

Rather than reducing price, increase what 450 AED includes — add the AI Reels Pack Standard (250 AED value) as a free inclusion on all 2-hour+ bookings for a limited 60-day period. The client perceives more value. You pay only the marginal cost of running Opus Clip. The hourly rate is untouched.

2

Summer Rate — Gulf Nationals Only

A 15% discount exclusively for Gulf national ID holders (Saudi, Kuwaiti, Emirati, Bahraini, Qatari) on first sessions — June through August. Segment-specific, time-limited. Does not impact the rate for other clients. Shows cultural awareness and activates the summer market directly.

2

Never Publish "Promotional" Pricing Publicly

Discounts for specific campaigns, segments, or first-time offers should live on dedicated landing pages — not on the main booking page. The main page must always show full pricing. If a prospect finds your offer through a specific campaign, the discounted page applies. Everyone else sees the standard rate.

3

On the Editing Price (1,000 AED/hour)

This is the most price-sensitive item in your lineup. At 1,000 AED/hour with a 4–6 business day turnaround, clients perceive it as expensive and slow. The AI Reels Add-On (250–450 AED) is strategically positioned to absorb the demand for quick social content at a much lower price point — protecting the premium editing product for clients who genuinely need full-episode production quality.
